The Tuition Fee of Growth

Universities send you the bill before the semester. Life sends it after. A short lesson on what becoming someone new actually costs — and why it is still the best money you will never see.

Verba Mind · Issue 24 Reading time · 5 min Level · B1–B2 Vocabulary · 6 words

When you enter a university, someone hands you a paper with a number on it. The tuition fee. You know the price before you begin. Growth is not so polite. Growth lets you start for free, and then quietly collects payment along the way — in comfort, in sleep, in old friendships, in the easy version of yourself you have to leave behind.

Many people say they want to change, but what they really want is the result of change without the cost of it. They want the language without the awkward years of speaking badly. They want the strong body without the early mornings. They want confidence without the embarrassing first attempts. Then, when the bill arrives, they feel regret — not because they failed, but because they never truly paid.

There is a difference between thinking about your past and drowning in it. Psychologists call the drowning rumination: replaying the same painful scene a hundred times, like a film you hate but cannot stop watching. Rumination is paying the tuition fee twice and receiving no education for it.

"Growth lets you start for free — and collects payment along the way."

Healthy minds choose a different habit: reflection. Reflection asks quiet, useful questions. What did this teach me? What will I do differently tomorrow? The event is the same; the mind that reads it is different. One mind pays and learns. The other only pays.

And yes — there will be loss. Every serious change demands a sacrifice. The student who studies while friends are at the café is making one. So is the young creator posting videos nobody watches yet. Sacrifice feels like losing. In reality, it is trading — exchanging a small comfortable present for a larger future.

What carries you through the trade is not motivation, which disappears like weather, but the ability to stand up again after each fall. That quality is called resilience, and it grows exactly the way muscles do: only under weight.

So become a disciplined payer. Pay daily, in small amounts: one page, one workout, one honest conversation, one paragraph of English. The fee never disappears. But people who pay it willingly receive something universities cannot print on any diploma — a self they are proud to live with.

Class is always in session. The only question is whether you are paying for growth, or paying for staying the same. Both are expensive. Only one is an investment.

About Verba Mind

Where English class meets a philosophy book.

Most English lessons teach you to order coffee. Verba Mind teaches you to talk about your life — your discipline, your doubts, your dreams — in clear, confident English.

Every lesson is a short philosophical essay written for B1–B2 readers. Inside each one, carefully chosen vocabulary is woven into real ideas, so new words attach to real feelings — the way memory actually works.

Verba Mind began as an Instagram page in Uzbekistan and grew into this journal. The belief behind it is simple: a language is learned fastest when it carries something worth saying.
